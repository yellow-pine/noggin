# C0 pan: partial-Mech gravity bound

The proposed rearward pan line cannot use a 1:1 PG001 drive. This is an early
layout bound, not the finished #902 load certification.

On `32a35a55`, the actual eye mechanism and cranium electronics, plus a proposed
16 g head-carried pan servo, total **360.877 g**. Their head-local centre of mass
is **(1.835, 17.907, -6.297) mm**. CadQuery solid volumes and volume centroids use
each `stages.Part` material density, following `mjcf.mass`; documented servo
masses remain 16 g. This uses the existing solid-print mass convention, without
an invented infill reduction. It excludes the jaw, mouth servo, replacement head
mount, anatomical Bones, Skin, wires and fasteners.

With the head tilted 20 degrees, the pan-axis gravity moment is

    abs(sin(tilt) * sum(mass_g * (x*cos(pan) - (y-axis_y)*sin(pan)))) / 10000

in kgf cm. Coordinates are head-local millimetres. The pan axis co-tilts with the
nod carrier; no spring, friction, acceleration or ideal balancing credit enters
this calculation.

| -45 degrees | 0.230922 | 0.496242 |
| -22.5 degrees | 0.112719 | 0.256309 |
| 0 degrees | 0.022644 | 0.022644 |

The existing PG001 profile at 5 V interpolates its published 4.8/6 V values to
**0.333333 kgf cm rated**, 1.033333 kgf cm stall. The working/rated limit is the
relevant continuous-hold comparison. The partial-Mech load already exceeds it
by 58.5 percent at the proposed C0 line. Moving the pan case between the head and
nod carrier does not remove the front head's gravity moment.

Each additional 100 g at head-local `(0, 0, z)` adds 0.185253 kgf cm at tilt20,
pan45 on the C0 line; its Z coordinate does not affect this axial moment.
A frictionless 2:1 reduction would leave only 0.138401 kgf cm beyond the partial
Mech, equivalent to about 74.7 g at that reference location. At an illustrative
80 percent transmission efficiency, almost no margin remains. These are
sensitivities, not accepted efficiency or final-load assumptions.

The largest included masses are the two solid eyeball cores (45.985 g each),
gimbal frame (50.681 g), tray (27.948 g), and two caps (18.554 g each). A real
structural redesign could change those masses; changing a density/infill number
without changing and certifying the part cannot establish feasibility.

The production pan axis remains on its original line while the complete
replacement actuator/transmission and cable-safe bearing interface are being
designed. The unresolved actuator/layout choice was escalated before further
1:1-drive work. Full anatomical/Skin loads, actual transmission losses, dynamics,
current, stability and strength remain #902 acceptance work.
