# Toon eye timing

Researched 2026-11-01. Classic cartoon timing for blinks, takes, glances and holds, in ms for noggin's servo eyes (20 ms step,
428 deg/s slew, lid rest->shut ~22 deg, eye up to ~25 deg). 1 frame at 24 fps = 41.7 ms; "on twos" = each drawing held 2 frames;
1 foot = 16 frames = 667 ms. "Second-hand" = only in a page citing the book; "inferred" = my reading or arithmetic.
Scans read: Williams 2001 https://archive.org/details/TheAnimatorsSurvivalKitRichardWilliams (printed page numbers), Thomas &
Johnston 1981 https://archive.org/details/TheIllusionOfLifeDisneyAnimation (unnumbered scan: chapter only), Blair 1994
https://archive.org/details/prestonblaircartoonanimationtext (transcription's page numbers), Lasseter 1987
https://www.ics.uci.edu/~fowlkes/class/ics162/exercises/lasseter.pdf

## 1. Blinks

- Williams' blink formula (p. 326, "Eyes"): 7 drawings, open (1), lid a little down (2), lid to the pupil (3), shut (4), mostly shut (5),
  half (6), open (7); "crude, but effective - works well on ones or twos". On ones: close 3 frames (125 ms), shut 1 frame (42 ms),
  open 3 frames (125 ms). On twos: 250 / 83 / 250 ms. Spacing charts (my reading, inferred): the close starts gently and takes its
  biggest step into shut (3->4); the open takes even steps then its biggest step last (6->7). No overshoot past open, no lift first.
  Same page: "move pupil down with lid as if it's heavy - forcing it down."
- Thomas & Johnston, ch. 16 "Animating Expressions and Dialogue", "Eye Blinks": with one inbetween, "slow out" of each extreme;
  "If the inbetween is drawn in the middle, there will be more pictures on the screen of a half open eye than of either extreme."
  "Blinks of disbelief, puzzlement, or wonder usually are shown with a slight squint"; stunned = staggered "flutter blink".
  "2 feet 8 frames (less than 2 seconds) is the maximum time a drawing can remain on the screen without movement" (1.67 s); a
  blink in the hold "recaptures the living quality". "Blinks are good on any shift of eye direction".
- Same chapter, dialogue rules 7-8: eyes alone in close-up start "at least three frames ahead of the accent" (125 ms; 1-2 frames
  for a soft accent, 4-5 = 167-208 ms for a strong one); a blink should "close ahead of the accent by three to four frames"
  (125-167 ms). Also as Ollie Johnston's "rules for expressions" (second-hand, https://flong.com/archive/blog/2009/johnstons-rules-of-expressions-for-eyes-and-time-travel/index.html).
- T&J ch. 17 (Snow White mourning): "Sad eyes, slow blinks, and a few tears were all that was needed." No frame counts.
- Blair p. 182: "an eye blinks (contracts), opens wide (expands), and then goes into normal pose" - the only primary I found for a
  reopen that overshoots rest and settles. No frame counts.
- Williams p. 87 (Ken Harris): on a head turn "The person will blink ... (Unless they're frightened - then the eyes will stay open.)"
- The "2 close, 1 shut, 3 open" often credited to Williams is second-hand; his printed formula is 3/1/3. Wink and double blink:
  no frame counts in any of the four books. Style.

## 2. Takes (surprise / eureka)

- Williams pp. 285-289: "A 'take' is an anticipation of an accent which then settles." Disney-type (16 frames, 667 ms): squash down
  1->5 on twos (4 fr, 167 ms) with the eyes squeezed shut, up 5->8 on ones (3 fr, 125 ms) to a stretched, wide-eyed accent that "is
  only there for 2 frames" (83 ms), then down 8->16 on twos (8 fr, 333 ms) with spacing bunched at the end; "#16 is the position we
  read". Warner-type (14 frames, 583 ms): down 1->7 on twos (250 ms), "pop" 7->8 (1 frame, 42 ms), down 8->14 on ones (250 ms).
  A 19-frame variant: "keep the eyes and mouth closed until the accent", up on ones over 6 frames (250 ms). Optional "slight up
  anticipation of the down anticipation" (p. 286). "These are standard timings" (p. 287).
- Williams p. 293 (Basil Rathbone): no squash, no stretch; the head "goes up to #4 (the accent) in 3 frames" (125 ms) "then cushions
  back to #11 in a virtual hold" (7 frames, 292 ms). A small, sharp take reads when everything else is still.
- Blair p. 152: the crouch "is always brief", the stretch "also brief", then back to normal; can be reversed (stretch first).
- Thomas & Johnston ch. 3 "Moving Hold": a pose was held "at least eight, maybe as many as sixteen" frames (333-667 ms); to keep it
  alive "You hit the pose, then drift on beyond to an even stronger pose ... his eyes open wider".

## 3. Accents, holds, spacing

- Williams p. 293: "you need at least 6 frames to read any accent" (250 ms); Tex Avery: "a minimum of 5 frames to read a hold" (208 ms).
- Williams p. 295: "A hard accent bounces back": ease out over 1-5, the accent is felt at 6 (208 ms in), then it bounces back over
  7-10 (167 ms), spacing closing up. "A soft accent continues" past the accent instead. Overshoot-and-settle is this hard accent.
- Williams pp. 49-51: ease in/out = classical "slowing in/out", Ken Harris "called it 'cushioning'"; "snap out" then "cushion in".
  Favouring (p. 289): an inbetween drawn near one extreme. T&J ch. 3 "Slow In and Slow Out": inbetweens near each extreme "and only
  one fleeting drawing halfway between"; "Too much of this gave a mechanical feel". Blair p. 196: "jump into a hold or ... cushion
  into it", keep a hold alive with "subtle secondary actions", know "when to move the eyes to anticipate coming movement".

## 4. Lasseter 1987 (Computer Graphics 21(4):35-44)

- Timing ladder, quoted from Thomas & Johnston: two head drawings with 0 inbetweens = "hit by a tremendous force", 1 = hit by a brick,
  2 = "nervous tic", 3 = dodging, 4 = crisp order, 5 = friendly, 6 = sees the car he wanted, 7 = better look, 8 = searching, 9 =
  "appraises, considering thoughtfully", 10 = "stretches a sore muscle". On ones that is (n+1) x 42 ms = 42-458 ms; on twos double.
  The text does not say ones or twos (inferred conversion).
- Anticipation: with the audience prepared an action "can be much faster"; Wally B.'s zip-off is "only 3 or 4 frames long" (125-167
  ms); "In a slow action the anticipation is often minimized." Follow-through: Luxo's arms snap together "two frames after the base
  lands" (83 ms); heavier parts "take longer to settle". Spline overshoot is named as a defect, not a device.

## 5. What 20 ms ticks and 428 deg/s allow (inferred arithmetic; servo acceleration, deadband and load not modelled)

- 1 frame = ~2 ticks, max 8.6 deg per tick. Lid 22 deg needs >= 52 ms (3 ticks, ~1.3 frames); eye 25 deg needs 58 ms. A 1-frame
  pop becomes ~1.5 frames: command a step and let the slew be the pop. Williams' blink on ones fits (last close step ~half the lid
  in one frame = ~265 deg/s). Easing needs ticks: a 125 ms move has 6 steps; easing inside a 1-2 frame move is lost.

## For noggin

Suggested toon timings. "Backed" = the numbers sit inside a source above; "style" = our choice, no source gives it.

- Blink (~600 ms): lift lids 2-3 deg over 60 ms (style; generalises Williams' "up anticipation", p. 286), close ~150 ms accelerating
  into shut (backed: Williams 125-250 ms, biggest step last), shut ~80 ms (backed: 1-2 frames), reopen ~250 ms (backed: 125-250 ms;
  Williams' open speeds up at the end, so an ease-out open is the natural-blink look, not his), overshoot 2-3 deg past rest then
  settle over ~120 ms (backed in kind by Blair p. 182; size and time are style). Land the shut 125-167 ms before the beat it marks.
- Wink: one lid closes ~200 ms, holds ~350 ms, reopens ~250 ms; other lid squints ~20% (style throughout; the hold length is
  backed by the 5-8 frame readability minimums, 208-333 ms).
- Take / eureka (~700 ms): lids squeeze shut over ~170 ms, pop to wide (past rest) at full slew (~60 ms), hold the accent ~80 ms,
  cushion back over ~330 ms to a still-wide read pose, then a moving hold drifting 1-2 deg wider for 300-600 ms (backed: Williams
  Disney take p. 286, T&J moving hold). Small take for "attention": rest to wide in ~125 ms, cushion back a third over ~300 ms
  (backed: Rathbone, p. 293).
- Glance > ~15 deg: 1-2 deg counter-move over ~60 ms (style; anticipation is backed as a principle, not for eyes), main move at slew,
  overshoot ~8-10% then settle back over ~170 ms (backed in kind: hard accent p. 295; size is style), blink during the move (backed:
  T&J, Williams p. 87). Soft glances (thinking) skip the overshoot and slow in (backed: soft accent). Lead any beat by ~125 ms.
- Drowsy / thinking slow blink: close 300-400 ms, shut 200-400 ms, open 500-800 ms to a lowered lid (style; T&J name "slow blinks"
  but give no numbers; the 9-10 inbetween rung, 417-917 ms, is "considering thoughtfully").
- Wince: squeeze shut at slew (~60 ms, "no inbetweens"), hold 300-500 ms, open in two staggered steps over ~250 ms (style; the
  squeezed-eye squash pose is backed by the take drawings, pp. 286-289).
- Holds: no pose still longer than ~1.6 s without a blink or drift (backed: T&J 40 frames); no accent shorter than ~250 ms (Williams).

Not reached: Illusion of Life page numbers (scan unnumbered); Whitaker & Halas "Timing for Animation" (Lasseter's ref. 30).
