# Web toon: three.js 0.186.1 for style B in the browser (2026-11-01, #194)

Question: what the page needs from three.js to draw style B (docs/model-notes.md) from Blender's GLB: the pin and its
integrity hashes, a two-step toon shadow with one highlight band, silhouette outlines and crease lines at the
pictures' 2.5 px, the look numbers carried in glTF extras, and the gestures through `AnimationMixer`. Rule: newest
release at least two weeks old.

Sources: the three.js files at `https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/three@0.186.1/` (`B/` below), the npm registry, MDN's
browser-compat-data (`html/elements/script.json`) and Blender 5.2's bundled `io_scene_gltf2`, all read 2026-11-01.

## Pin: three 0.186.1

- `npm view three time`: 0.186.1 published 2026-09-24 (38 days old); 0.187.0 is 2026-10-30, under the two-week rule.
- `B/build/three.module.js` imports `./three.core.js`, so both need a hash. The addons import the bare `three` and
  their siblings: `examples/jsm/loaders/GLTFLoader.js` imports `../utils/BufferGeometryUtils.js` and
  `../utils/SkeletonUtils.js`; `lines/LineSegments2.js` imports `./LineSegmentsGeometry.js` and `./LineMaterial.js`;
  `controls/OrbitControls.js` and `loaders/DRACOLoader.js` only `three`. Ten files in all, each hashed with
  `openssl dgst -sha384 -binary <file> | base64` (the values are in web/index.html's import map).
- Import maps carry `integrity` per URL: Chrome 127, Firefox 138, Safari 18 (MDN BCD
  `html.elements.script.type.importmap.integrity`); every module the page imports, directly or through an addon, is
  checked against it. Older browsers ignore the key and load unchecked.
- `DRACOLoader` fetches its decoder (`draco_wasm_wrapper.js`, `draco_decoder.wasm`) with `FileLoader` from
  `setDecoderPath` (`B/examples/jsm/loaders/DRACOLoader.js#_loadLibrary`), so no integrity check reaches it; the wasm
  path needs only those two (`draco_decoder.js` is the no-WebAssembly fallback). sha256 of 0.186.1's
  `libs/draco/gltf/` copies: wrapper `8bb2952d...d76f6`, wasm `a680d927...df8ac` (`shasum -a 256`; full values in
  web/build.py).

## Toon shading: `MeshToonMaterial` and its `gradientMap`

- `gradientmap_pars_fragment`: `coord = dot(normal, lightDir) * 0.5 + 0.5`; with a gradient map the irradiance is
  `texture2D(gradientMap, coord).r` (without one, a built-in 0.7/1.0 step at coord 0.7). A `DataTexture` with
  `NearestFilter` gives hard steps.
- `lights_toon_pars_fragment`: `RE_Direct_Toon` adds `gradient * light.color * diffuse / PI`, so a single
  `DirectionalLight` of intensity `PI` and no ambient light yield `colour * gradient` exactly: Blender's toon pass
  (base colour times a constant ramp) in the browser.
- What the pictures actually show (a probe: `model/render/blender.py`'s `setup()` and `material()` on a smooth UV
  sphere, orthographic, the key light placed as the pictures place it, pixels read back against N.L and N.H): the
  final colour over the part colour, linear, is 0.40 where N.L <= 0 and rises smoothly to 0.76 at N.L 0.95, i.e.
  `TOON * SHADOW + (1 - TOON) * (SUN / PI) * N.L` within 1% (0.396 + 0.378 N.L). The toon ramp never reaches its lit
  step: the 0.8 grey Diffuse BSDF under sun 2.6 peaks at 0.8 * 2.6 / PI = 0.66, under `SHADOW_AT` 0.7; and the world
  (strength 0.4) adds under 1% in the background render. The highlight band sets in between N.H 0.978 and 0.984.
  So the page's gradient map is that curve, sampled from Blender's numbers at export time, not a two-texel step.
- No specular term: a highlight band needs a few lines through `onBeforeCompile` before `#include <opaque_fragment>`,
  where `outgoingLight`, `normal`, `geometryViewDir` (`lights_fragment_begin`) and `directionalLights[0]` are in scope.

## Outlines and creases

- `examples/jsm/effects/OutlineEffect.js` `calculateOutline` offsets each vertex by
  `normalize(pos - projectionMatrix * modelViewMatrix * (position + normal))`, i.e. it adds a unit normal in the
  object's own units. Our GLBs are in metres and the Mech is 0.1 m, so that point lands a metre away, behind the
  camera for close views; the effect assumes scenes in larger units. An inverted hull of our own (back faces, black,
  pushed out along the view-space normal by a pixel width in clip space) is a few shader lines.
- The parts carry split normals at sharp edges (weighted normals, sharp from 35 degrees): a hull pushed along them
  opens at every hard corner. `BufferGeometryUtils.mergeVertices` (tolerance 1e-4) on positions only, then
  `computeVertexNormals`, gives the hull smooth normals.
- `EdgesGeometry(geometry, thresholdAngle)`: an edge is kept when the angle between its faces' normals exceeds
  `thresholdAngle` degrees (default 1); vertices are matched on positions rounded to 4 decimals (0.1 mm in metres), so
  split-normal seams join. 60 is Freestyle's crease rule (`crease_angle` 120 degrees between the faces).
- `LineBasicMaterial.linewidth` is 1 px on WebGL; `lines/LineSegments2` with `LineMaterial` draws widths in pixels
  (`linewidth`, needs `resolution`). `LineSegmentsGeometry.fromEdgesGeometry` takes the crease edges.

## glTF extras and animation

- Blender exporter (`io_scene_gltf2/blender/exp/gather.py` `__gather_extras`): with `export_extras=True` the scene's
  custom properties become the glTF scene's `extras` (also objects', materials' and meshes').
- `GLTFLoader` `assignExtrasToUserData` copies `extras` into `userData` for scenes, nodes, meshes, materials and
  animations, so the scene's look is `gltf.scene.userData`. A material's `baseColorFactor` arrives as `material.color`
  (linear), the part colour Blender already exports.
- `AnimationMixer`: `clipAction(clip)`, `setLoop(LoopOnce)`, `clampWhenFinished`, and the mixer dispatches
  `finished` with `{action}` when a finite action ends (three.core.js `AnimationAction._updateTime`).
